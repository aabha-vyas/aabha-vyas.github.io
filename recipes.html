Recipe Recommender

CS 410content-based recommendation with NLP · Python, scikit-learn, NLTK · with Priscilla Yuan

Most recipe search matches keywords. We built a recommender that starts from a recipe you already like and finds similar ones, then re-ranks them by what you care about: fewer ingredients, fewer steps or a healthier dish.

It's a content-based recommender. Each recipe becomes a vector built from its own text, and recommendations come from how close those vectors are. No user ratings are needed.

how it works

1. clean the text

We removed duplicate recipes, stripped measurements like "1 cup" and "3 tbsp," and joined multi-word ingredients into single tokens, so "soy sauce" becomes soy_sauce and isn't confused with other sauces. Cooking steps were split into sentences with NLTK.

2. turn recipes into vectors

Each recipe's title and ingredients become a TF-IDF vector with single words and word pairs. TF-IDF boosts distinctive ingredients like salmon or ginger and downweights ones every recipe has, like salt and oil. A second TF-IDF vector covers the cooking steps.

3. find and rank matches

Cosine similarity finds the 50 closest recipes, weighted 85% on ingredients and 15% on steps. Those candidates are then re-ranked by a blend of 70% similarity and 30% the user's preference, so a slightly less similar recipe can win if it's much simpler.

4. explain the results

Each recommendation shows a 0–100 match score, the ingredients it adds that your recipe doesn't have, and a two-sentence summary of the steps. The summary picks the sentences with the highest TF-IDF scores.

what we learned

  • Raw cosine scores for good matches only reached about 0.20–0.40, which looks weak to users, so we show a match bar and a 0–100 score instead.
  • Duplicates survived basic cleaning and filled results with the same recipe, so we also removed recipes with identical ingredients and steps after parsing.
  • Ingredient lists were messy: separators varied, and small items like salt and pepper often showed up in the directions instead.

limits

  • We judged recommendations by reading them. We didn't measure them against labeled data.
  • The healthiness score is a simple keyword count, not real nutrition data.
