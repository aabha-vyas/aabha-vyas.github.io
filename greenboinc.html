GreenBOINC

Fall 2025carbon-aware scheduling for volunteer computing

BOINC lets millions of volunteers donate spare computer time to science. It splits big research workloads into small tasks and sends them to laptops and desktops around the world. Cloud providers already schedule work around the power grid to cut emissions. BOINC doesn't do this.

GreenBOINC adds carbon-aware scheduling to BOINC. Volunteer machines run at different times and sit on different power grids, so the same work can be done with less carbon by choosing when and where it runs.

read the paper (PDF)

how it works

when: temporal shifting

Each volunteer chooses how much of the day to donate. GreenBOINC checks the carbon forecast for their grid and runs BOINC during the cleanest hours instead of whenever the machine is idle.

where: locality shifting

A central coordinator knows which projects each volunteer can run and how clean their grid is. It sends the most power-hungry tasks to volunteers on cleaner grids while every project still gets the compute time it asked for.

results

18%
lower emissions with both schedulers, at 75% uptime
15.9%
from choosing where tasks run
up to 12.5%
from choosing when they run, at 75% uptime

Results come from a simulation of 500 volunteers placed where BOINC's computing actually happens, using a year of real grid data. The less of the day volunteers donate, the more room there is to pick clean hours: savings reach 22% at 50% uptime.

Where a volunteer lives matters a lot. Timing alone saves 12.5% in the Netherlands but under 1% in Iceland, whose grid is already clean all day.

limits

  • Scheduling decisions come from the real system, but task execution and emissions are simulated.
  • It assumes sub-projects can be picked automatically, which BOINC doesn't support yet.

Methods, setup and full results are in the paper.
